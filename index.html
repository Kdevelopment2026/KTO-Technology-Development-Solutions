04 How I work

Six stages, and you see something real at every one

  1. 01

    Discover

    What is going wrong, for whom, and how you will know it is fixed. I speak to the people doing the work, not only the people commissioning the training.

  2. 02

    Define

    Objectives, scope, format and success measures agreed in writing, so later decisions have something to be measured against.

  3. 03

    Design

    A storyboard covering every screen: on-screen text, narration, interaction and feedback. Subject-matter experts review the words before anything is built.

  4. 04

    Develop

    Build, media production and accessibility work run together. A working prototype of the hardest screen comes first, not last.

  5. 05

    Review

    Two structured rounds with consolidated comments, plus a pilot with real learners on the real platform. Late surprises usually come from skipping this.

  6. 06

    Deliver

    Packaged, tested in your LMS and handed over with source files and a maintenance note, so your team can make the next change without me.

Working with your people

Subject-matter experts get a storyboard to react to rather than a blank page. It is far quicker to correct a draft than to dictate one. Stakeholders get fixed review points with a single consolidated comment set, so nobody is chasing versions. Learners get involved at pilot, on the device and platform they will actually use, because that is where the problems show up.

06 About

I turn the content nobody reads into something people use

I am a training designer, instructional designer and e-learning developer with over 25 years of experience creating innovative learning solutions. I specialise in transforming complex information into clear, engaging and accessible digital learning that supports real workplace performance.

I have worked with clients across medical, HR, compliance, insurance, education and finance, designing courses, simulations, videos and learning resources for very different audiences. Telecommunications training and soft-skills development sit in there too, and the pattern is always the same: a policy, a system or a procedure that matters, written for the people who wrote it rather than the people who have to follow it. The design job is to find the decisions inside that content and build the practice around them.

I am interested in what emerging technology can genuinely do for learning, and I blend traditional instructional design with AI-assisted approaches such as drafting, localisation and grounded support assistants, where they earn their place. Regulated environments are normal for me, so accessibility, auditability and evidence of completion are not afterthoughts: I build to WCAG 2.2 AA as standard, test with a screen reader rather than assuming, and hand over source files your team can maintain.
